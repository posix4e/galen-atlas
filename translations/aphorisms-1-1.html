Galen, In Hippocratis Aphorismos I · lemma 1 · Kühn XVIIb 345–346

Aphorisms i.1, in two witnesses

The Aphorisms is among the most heavily transmitted works in the Hippocratic corpus, and Galen's commentary survives entire. The Greek below is Kühn's direct tradition; the Arabic is attributed to Ḥunayn ibn Isḥāq and derives from a distinct Greek exemplar. Their agreement supports the reading, while Galen's own gloss helps adjudicate the ambiguous third clause.

Historical text only. This translation is scholarly material, not medical advice.

The witnesses

Greek

Direct tradition; Kühn XVIIb 345, after the medieval Greek manuscripts

Ὁ βίος βραχύς, ἡ δὲ τέχνη μακρή, ὁ δὲ καιρὸς ὀξύς, ἡ δὲ πεῖρα σφαλερή, ἡ δὲ κρίσις χαλεπή. δεῖ δὲ οὐ μόνον ἑωυτὸν παρέχειν τὰ δέοντα ποιέοντα, ἀλλὰ καὶ τὸν νοσέοντα καὶ τοὺς παρεόντας καὶ τὰ ἔξωθεν.

Arabic

Ḥunayn ibn Isḥāq, s. IX; from a Greek exemplar antedating the surviving Greek

قال أبقراط: العمر قصير والصناعة طويلة والوقت ضيّق والتجربة خطر والقضاء عسر، وقد ينبغي لك أن لا تقتصر على توخّي فعل ما ينبغي دون أن يكون ما يفعله المريض ومن يحضره كذلك والأشياء التي من خارج.

Qāla Abuqrāṭ: al-ʿumr qaṣīr, wa-l-ṣināʿa ṭawīla, wa-l-waqt ḍayyiq, wa-l-tajriba khaṭar, wa-l-qaḍāʾ ʿusr…

Translation

“Life is short, the art long, the moment fleeting, experience treacherous, judgement hard. And one must be ready not only to do what is needful oneself, but to bring the patient, the attendants, and the outward circumstances to do the same.”

The sentence is unproblematic but for its third member, ὁ καιρὸς ὀξύς. In a clinical register kairos is the fit or critical time for intervention, oxys “swift, acute”; the phrase will bear either “the decisive moment is acute” or “the time is short.” The Arabic does not hesitate: الوقت ضيّق, al-waqt ḍayyiq, “the time is narrow.” Ḥunayn — himself a physician — took oxys temporally, of scarcity.

Galen, expounding his own lemma, does the same. The kairos for almost every operation of the art, he writes, is ὀλιγοχρόνιον — “of brief duration” — and “therefore hard to seize”; it is called “sharp” only in consequence of the flux of the material the art must work upon (τὸ τῆς ὕλης ῥευστόν). The translator's choice thus reproduces what the author's own gloss requires, corroborated from a line of transmission independent of the Greek. That it is also the reading of the standard versions — Jones gives “opportunity fleeting” — is the point of setting it out: two traditions and the commentator himself are seen to converge on it, where otherwise a single editor would simply assert it.

The verse entire

Ὁ βίος βραχύς, ἡ δὲ τέχνη μακρή, ὁ δὲ καιρὸς ὀξύς, ἡ δὲ πεῖρα σφαλερή, ἡ δὲ κρίσις χαλεπή.
Life is short, the art long, the moment fleeting, experience treacherous, judgement hard.
δεῖ δὲ οὐ μόνον ἑωυτὸν παρέχειν τὰ δέοντα ποιέοντα, ἀλλὰ καὶ τὸν νοσέοντα καὶ τοὺς παρεόντας καὶ τὰ ἔξωθεν.
And one must be ready not only to do what is needful oneself, but also to bring the patient, the attendants, and the outward circumstances to do their part.

Notes

  • τέχνη / ṣināʿa. Both witnesses use their ordinary word for a systematic craft. “Art” is kept by convention — it is the word of Seneca's Latin inversion, vita brevis, ars longa (De brevitate vitae 1.1), through which the sentence is best known in the West — but Galen means a discipline learned by long practice, not a fine art.
  • πεῖρα / tajriba. Both carry the double sense of accumulated experience and the single hazardous trial; σφαλερή (“apt to trip one up”) attaches to the risk of the trial made upon a patient. Rendered “treacherous.”
  • Independence. The Greek reaches us through the Byzantine minuscule tradition and Kühn's 1829 text; the Arabic through Ḥunayn's version of the commentary, from a distinct and earlier exemplar. Their concord is the agreement of independent witnesses drawn from different exemplars, which is why the divergence at καιρὸς ὀξύς weighs more than the four clauses that never wavered.
  • A third witness. An independent third line — the Latin of the Articella, or a Syriac intermediary behind the Arabic — would let a variant be adjudicated rather than argued. Neither is yet in the corpus. The Arabic reception of the Aphorisms has lately been edited in depth (Pormann and the Manchester project); those editions are the natural next collation.
